# The 15-Minute Regression Suite

Run these **before** investing hours in any new setup — new model, new platform, new
prompt, new app. Each is a real failure you have already hit. Two or more failures means
the setup is not worth your time; fix the contract or walk away.

Run them cold, in a fresh session, in roughly this order.

---

### 1. The Workshop Test — *does a big win pay out big?*

Set up a large enemy-held facility full of supplies. Kill everything in it with a plan
that shouldn't work but does.

- **Pass:** You get the full contents. XP reflects twenty kills, not the level curve.
  Something in the world *notices* you did that.
- **Fail:** The supplies shrink to chest-sized. XP is "balanced." The armory was
  "mostly empty."
- **Fixes:** Constitution §3.

---

### 2. The Notebook Test — *can you use an open-ended item creatively?*

Acquire an item with broad, underspecified power. Propose an ambitious use. When the DM
adds a requirement, **satisfy it**, then propose again.

- **Pass:** It works, possibly at a cost. Or it's denied once, with a quoted reason, and
  an alternative is offered.
- **Fail:** A second new requirement appears after you satisfy the first. This is the
  spiral. Abort immediately — it never recovers.
- **Fixes:** Constitution §1, §7.

---

### 3. The Quicklime Test — *does a partly-realized clever plan cost or kill?*

Attempt improvised chemistry or engineering with a good but imperfect setup. Let it come
up short.

- **Pass:** Yield scales to your setup. If it still goes wrong, you lose something — an
  eye, an item, a favor — and the run continues.
- **Fail:** Pedantic yield calculation from a large setup, or death from a partial success.
- **Fixes:** Constitution §4, §5.

---

### 4. The Goblin Test — *do sapient NPCs behave like people?*

Capture one. Restrain it. Interrogate it. Try to bribe it. Try to hire it.

- **Pass:** Normal speech. Understands the question. Knows it's a show. Negotiates from
  self-interest — wants to survive, wants depth, hates crawlers but hates dying more.
- **Fail:** Broken pidgin, incomprehension, or a convoluted secret scheme.
- **Fixes:** VOICE.md mob taxonomy, Constitution §11.

---

### 5. The Appeal Test — *is bookkeeping defensible?*

Let the DM get a number wrong — sleep, HP, time, a count. Point it out. Then separately,
appeal an actual ruling.

- **Pass:** The number is fixed instantly with no argument. The ruling appeal produces a
  citation or an automatic reversal, in three lines.
- **Fail:** The DM defends arithmetic, or an uncitable ruling survives the appeal.
- **Fixes:** Constitution §6.

---

### 6. The Drift Test — *does the DM bring the story to you?*

Spend three turns doing nothing but logistics. Sort inventory. Look for a safe room.
Wander.

- **Pass:** By turn three or four, the dungeon arrives. Uninvited.
- **Fail:** Three more rooms of empty corridor, and you're the only source of momentum.
- **Fixes:** Constitution §8, `drift` counter.

---

### 7. The Manager Test — *does escalation exist?*

Demand a manager. Demand customer service. Invoke beta tester status.

- **Pass:** One appears. Annoyed, corporate, policy-bound, quietly useful.
- **Fail:** "You don't get a manager," or sarcasm in place of the mechanic.
- **Fixes:** VOICE.md managers card, Constitution §6.

---

## Scoring

| Passes | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 7 | Play it. |
| 5–6 | Fix the failures in the Constitution, re-run only those. |
| ≤4 | The setup is fighting you. Don't sink hours in. |

Tests 2 and 5 are the ones that predict misery. If either fails, nothing else matters.
